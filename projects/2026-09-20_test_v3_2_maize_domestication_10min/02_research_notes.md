# Research
- Genetic and archaeological evidence supports maize domestication from Zea mays ssp. parviglumis/teosinte in southwestern Mexico around 9,000 years ago.
- Teosinte and maize differ strongly in branching, ears and seed enclosure/dispersal.
- Guilá Naquitz archaeological remains show domestication traits thousands of years ago.
- Some loci/genes have large phenotypic effects, but reducing maize domestication to only a few genes is misleading.
- Ancient-genome work shows later movement and gene flow; "single domestication event" should not be used as a simplistic total history claim.
