# 02 — Research Notes

Reused from the v3 cannabis project because the v3.1 rerun is testing editorial/integrity behavior, not replacing the evidence base.

Key evidence:

- Ren et al. 2021: 110 whole-genome Cannabis samples; East Asia central to domestication history; important divergence estimate around 12,000 BP with a wide confidence interval; early crop likely multipurpose; stronger fiber-vs-psychoactive specialization later, around 4,000 BP.
- Zhuzhai: early charred Cannabis seed around 7,900–7,600 years ago; consistent with early cultivation but not proof of fully domesticated status.
- Early Japan evidence complicates a single-point story but does not prove an independent domestication event.
- Liu et al. 2026, Shandong: microscopic mineral plant remains support Cannabis integration into local agriculture around 4,500–3,400 BP.
- Haimenkou: >800 Cannabis grains, mostly within overlapping size ranges of modern functional groups; useful evidence for diversification rather than clean binary categories.
- Yanghai: preserved Cannabis remains in a burial context.
- Jirzankal 2019: chemical residue evidence around 500 BCE; CBN and related signals support deliberate burning of Cannabis containing relatively high psychoactive compounds.

Hard caveats:
- ~12,000 years is a model estimate, not an excavated exact date.
- do not generalize regional evidence to all populations.
- do not claim every ancient Cannabis find was psychoactive use.
- do not claim Jirzankal alone proves deliberate breeding for high THC.
