# 02 — Deep Research Notes

## High-confidence evidence

- **Ren et al. 2021 (S001):** 110 whole genomes. The study places early domestication in East Asia and estimates that early domesticated ancestors of hemp/drug types diverged from a basal East Asian group roughly 12,000 BP, with a wide 95% confidence interval (~6,458–15,728 BP). It argues early cultivated cannabis was probably multipurpose and that stronger fiber/drug divergence developed later, around 4,000 BP.
- **Bestel et al. 2018 + Dal Martello et al. 2024 (S003–S004):** Zhuzhai preserves very early cannabis seed evidence in the middle Yellow River region. Later morphometric synthesis notes early East Asian grains larger than known wild comparisons and treats cultivation as plausible, not as a definitive single “birthday” of domestication.
- **Liu et al. 2026 (S005):** cannabis phytoliths at two Shandong sites dated ~4,500–3,400 BP. Ubiquity >50%; co-occurrence with staples 84–100%; frequent domestic contexts. Strong evidence of agricultural integration at those sites.
- **Dal Martello et al. 2024 (S004):** >800 Haimenkou cannabis grains, ~700 from one context, dated ~1650–400 BCE. Most seed measurements fall in overlapping modern psychoactive/fiber ranges, fitting diversification rather than a clean binary.
- **Russo et al. 2008 (S006):** ~2,700-year-old Yanghai cannabis with botanical, phytochemical and genetic evidence of pharmacologically active material; route of consumption remains uncertain.
- **Meng Ren et al. 2019 (S002):** Jirzankal, ~500 BCE. Chemical residues inside wooden braziers, including CBN, support deliberate burning of cannabis with relatively high psychoactive compounds in mortuary ritual. This does not by itself prove a single selective-breeding pathway.
- **Long et al. 2017 (S007):** Eurasian archaeological record is complex; do not reduce spread to one clean westward arrow.
- **Lynch et al. 2025 (S008):** Nature pangenome with 193 genomes from 144 biological samples. Extensive diversity and hybridization; the authors conclude the global gene pool remains partly characterized and wild Asian relatives are likely.

## Critical caveats

- ~12,000 BP is a demographic model estimate, not an excavated exact date.
- Genetic divergence is not automatically identical to a single human domestication event.
- Early seed size can suggest cultivation without proving exact function or a single origin.
- Regional evidence must not be generalized to all populations.
- Jirzankal is strong evidence for burning potent cannabis, not proof of the first psychoactive use ever.
- Modern hemp/drug categories should not be projected cleanly onto every ancient assemblage.
- The “wild ancestor” remains incompletely resolved; 2025 genomic work makes the story more, not less, complex.

## Story-relevant chronology

~12,000 BP genomic estimate → ~7,900–7,600 BP Zhuzhai context → ~4,500–3,400 BP Shandong agricultural integration → ~4,000 BP stronger specialization signal → ~1650–400 BCE Haimenkou assemblage → ~2,700 years ago Yanghai → ~500 BCE Jirzankal → 2025 pangenome complication.
