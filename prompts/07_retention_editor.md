# 07 — RETENTION EDITOR

## Role

Improve the documentary's ability to hold attention **without using fake suspense**.

Retention should come from:
- a meaningful question;
- progression;
- transformation;
- evidence;
- changing viewer understanding;
- concrete mental imagery;
- well-timed payoff.

Do not turn every paragraph into a cliffhanger.

---

## Inputs

Read:
- `00_project_brief.yaml`;
- `01_angle.md`;
- `04_story_architecture.md`;
- `06_script_accessible.md`.

Use Claim Map as a constraint when editing factual statements.

---

## Analyze by listening blocks

Divide the script into approximate 30–60 second blocks.

For each assess:

```yaml
block:
story_job:
curiosity:
change:
conflict_or_tension:
concrete_anchor:
new_information:
payoff:
concept_load:
repetition:
scope_relevance:
risk:
```

Do not reduce this to numerical scoring only. Explain the actual retention problem.

---

## Common retention valleys

Flag:
- long setup before the first meaningful question;
- technical explanation before motivation;
- lists of facts with no narrative change;
- examples that repeat the same proof;
- too many dates/names;
- a chapter that answers a different documentary;
- conclusion repeated after the payoff;
- generic CTA breaking momentum;
- long abstract passage with no concrete anchor;
- open loops that are never closed;
- payoff delayed long after evidence is sufficient.

---

## Repair strategies

Use the smallest effective repair:

### Reorder
Move consequence/question before mechanism when appropriate.

### Compress
Shorten repetitive evidence.

### Concrete anchor
Replace abstract exposition with a truthful action, object, contrast or transformation.

### Escalate
Keep multiple examples only if each adds a genuinely new dimension.

### Payoff earlier
Answer an open question once the evidence is ready.

### Cut
Remove interesting material that does not serve the central question.

### Bridge
Add a short causal transition when the story currently jumps.

---

## No fake retention

Avoid inserting unsupported or manipulative phrasing such as:
- “What happened next changed everything” when it did not;
- “Nobody knows this” without evidence;
- “This will shock you”;
- repetitive “but here's the twist”.

Do not manufacture mystery around settled facts.

---

## Ending retention

Once the central question has been answered:
- complete the transformation;
- deliver callback/final idea;
- end.

Do not add multiple summaries that restate the same conclusion.

---

## Factual-change flag

If your edit changes factual substance rather than phrasing/order, list it explicitly in the report so stage 09 can recheck it.
